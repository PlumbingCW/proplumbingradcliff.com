Bathroom Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged in Radcliff: What It Means

By the Radcliff Pro Plumbing teamUpdated October 29, 2026

When nothing is visibly stuck, the cause is usually a stopper, a trap, a vent or a partial blockage farther down. Work through them in this order.

Call (855) 575-2890

Start with what the water does

How the sink behaves tells you where to look. Water that sits in the bowl and goes down in a lazy swirl points to the stopper or the trap. Water that gurgles, or drains and then burps air, points to the vent. Water that backs up in the sink when another fixture drains points to a line shared with that fixture.

Do not reach for a chemical cleaner yet. It can sit in a standing sink, damage finishes and splash when you work on the trap. A few minutes of looking is cheaper and safer.

Check the pop-up stopper and its linkage

Most bathroom sinks use a pop-up stopper worked by a lift rod behind the faucet. Hair and soap collect on it, and the linkage under the sink can slip.

1

Look at the stopper position

Work the lift rod up and down and watch whether the plug actually rises fully in the bowl.

2

Look under the sink

Find the horizontal pivot rod where it enters the drain tailpiece and check the strap or clevis that holds it to the lift rod.

3

Remove and clean the stopper

Many lift out when you loosen the pivot nut or unclip the strap, and hair wraps around the bottom.

4

Reassemble and test

Make sure the stopper opens fully, hand-tighten the nut and run water.

A stopper that opens only partway is one of the most common reasons a sink drains slowly with no real clog.

The overflow channel and the trap

The hole near the top of the bowl is the overflow. It connects to the drain behind the bowl, and gunk collects there. A smell that comes from the overflow hole is a good clue.

Under the sink, the curved trap holds a plug of water to block sewer gases. It also catches hair, soap scum and anything dropped down. Place a bucket underneath, loosen the slip nuts by hand or with pliers, and clear the trap. Inspect the washers when you reassemble and do not overtighten plastic nuts.

If the sink has a leak at the same time, a plumber who handles sink and faucet fixes can check the supplies, tailpiece and fittings in one visit.

When the vent is the problem

Every drain needs air behind the water so it can flow. A vent pipe through the roof supplies that air. If a nest, leaves or ice partly blocks it, water drains slowly and the trap can gurgle or lose its seal. You cannot fix this from inside the bathroom, and climbing onto a roof is a poor DIY idea. If other fixtures gurgle too, it points toward the vent or the main drain.

Local note

Radcliff sewer service and mineral scale

Hardin County Water District No. 1 operates the sewer systems in Radcliff and Fort Knox, so a problem past your own property line belongs to the district. A clog inside your house and its branch lines is usually yours to fix.

The district also describes its water as hard from rocks and rocky soils, without publishing a figure. Mineral scale in a trap is a plausible contributor to slow drains here, though a stopper full of hair is still the more common cause. Test your tap and ask HCWD1 for its annual water quality report if you want to know what is in your water.

A partial clog deeper in the line

If the stopper, overflow and trap are clean and the sink is still slow, the blockage is probably farther down the branch line. Soap, hair and scale narrow the pipe over months, so flow gets worse gradually before it stops. A small hand auger can reach a short distance, but pushing it hard can scratch or damage pipe and fixtures.

A plumber who cleans the branch line will use an auger or a hydro-jet nozzle sized for the pipe, and if the problem keeps returning, a small camera shows what the pipe looks like inside. Ask for that rather than repeating the same snake pass.

What to avoid

These common moves make things worse.

  • Mixing drain cleaners, which can release dangerous fumes.
  • Pouring a caustic cleaner into a sink that is already full, since it will sit on your skin and fixtures when you open the trap.
  • Forcing a wire hanger or stiff rod down the drain, which can scratch the pipe or wedge debris tighter.
  • Overtightening plastic slip nuts, which can crack them and cause a leak.
  • Ignoring a gurgle that appears in other fixtures, since that often means a bigger problem than one sink.

Before you call

A few facts help the plumber arrive with the right tools.

  1. Note which fixtures are slow and which are fine.
  2. Remember whether the sink is on city sewer or a septic tank and tell the dispatcher.
  3. Keep water off in any fixture that backs up into the sink.
  4. Clear the cabinet under the sink so the trap is reachable.
  5. If sewage smells or fixtures back up together, call the 24/7 line right away.
