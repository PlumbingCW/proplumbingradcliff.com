Limestone ground, heavy rain and your drains

The USGS studied the limestone terrane around Elizabethtown because groundwater there moves through openings in the rock. In karst ground, rain can travel underground quickly. A floor drain that backs up only during long storms points to the sewer lateral or outside water, not a kitchen clog, so mention the timing when you call.

The Kentucky Geological Survey says never to use sinkholes as dumps. Hardin County water comes largely from springs, and what goes into the ground can reach them. Keep paint, oil and solvents out of yard drains too, and take them to proper disposal.

Ice storm weeks and frozen lines

The regional hazard plan records ice storms in the Hardin County zone in November 2018 and February 2021. Long power outages leave houses unheated. That is when pipes in crawl spaces, garages and outside walls freeze. Find your main shutoff valve before winter and show every adult in the house how to turn it.

When a line freezes, open the faucet it feeds so melting water has somewhere to go. Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, kept away from water on the floor. Never use an open flame. If a pipe has split, close the main and call the 24/7 line, because a burst often shows itself only once the ice melts.

Before we arrive

Ten minutes of setup keeps damage small and lets the plumber start sooner.
