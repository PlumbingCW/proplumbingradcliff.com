Spring water, sediment and your water heater

HCWD1 draws Vine Grove's water from Pirtle Spring, a groundwater source, and the district says the water is hard because it passes through rock and rocky soil. Hard water leaves mineral scale in the bottom of a tank heater. That layer causes the popping or rumbling many owners hear, and it makes the burner or lower element run longer for the same hot water.

No hardness figure is published for the system, so test your own tap with a simple kit and ask HCWD1 for its annual water quality report. The result tells you whether a yearly flush is enough or a softener is worth discussing. A plumber can flush the tank during a water heater repair visit and check the anode rod while the water is off.

Repair makes sense when one part has failed, such as a thermostat, element, thermocouple, gas control or relief valve. A tank that leaks from its own shell cannot be patched. Then replacing the heater is the only safe fix, and the plumber goes over tank size and fuel type with you before pricing it.

How to run the HCWD1 meter test

HCWD1 publishes a simple leak check for houses on its system, and every Vine Grove home is one of them. Plan on up to an hour without using water.

1

Shut everything off

Turn off every faucet, fixture and water-using appliance, and remember the ice maker.

2

Read the meter

Write down the number and look at the red leak indicator arrow, which turns when water is flowing.

3

Wait

Leave the water unused for 30 to 60 minutes, then read the meter a second time.

4

Compare

Any change between the two readings means water is leaving the pipes somewhere past the meter.

Small leaks add up. HCWD1 figures say a running toilet can waste up to 4,000 gallons a day, a dripping faucet up to 5,000 gallons a month, and a steady stream up to 21,000 gallons a month. If the toilets and faucets are quiet, our leak detection work uses acoustic listening and thermal imaging to locate leaks under floors and inside walls before anything is opened.

The district can review a qualifying leak for a bill adjustment, but only after the cause of the usage past the meter has been found. Keep the plumber's written finding and the repair invoice for that request. The rules are in the HCWD1 tariff, so ask the office about your own case.

Ice brings down power, and a house without heat can fall below freezing inside during a long outage. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces and unheated garages go first.

Before a storm, find the main shutoff and make sure it turns. Open sink cabinets on outside walls and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. If the power stays off and you plan to leave, closing the main and draining the faucets protects the pipes better than hoping the heat returns.
