Garbage disposal repair is the work of finding why a disposal will not grind, drain or seal, then fixing the motor, the jam, the seals or the connections. Leak under the sink: what to do while you wait

Water in a cabinet can swell the floor and reach the wall behind it. These emergency steps limit that.

  1. Turn off the disposal switch, then the breaker if the area is wet.
  2. Place a bucket or bowl under the leak and empty it often.
  3. Close the angle stops under the faucet if the leak seems to come from the supply lines.
  4. Remove cleaners and stored items from the cabinet and dry the floor with towels.
  5. Take photos of the leak and the damage in case your insurer asks.
  6. Run no water or dishwasher cycle until the leak source is found.

What is safe to try and what is not

Start with the power off. A disposal that is only jammed often frees up with a few simple steps.

  • Turn off the switch, then unplug the unit or flip the breaker before touching anything.
  • Use the hex key socket at the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth to free the flywheel.
  • Fish out visible objects with tongs or pliers and never with your hand or fingers.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit once the jam is clear.
  • Skip chemical drain cleaners, which can sit in the unit, splash and eat seals.
  • Do not run water through a unit that is leaking, and do not reach into the chamber for any reason.

If the unit still hums after a clean reset, the motor or its capacitor may have failed, which is a repair-or-replace call.

Flange leaks and drain-line leaks are different jobs

A leak at the sink flange shows up at the ring where the disposal meets the sink, and it is fixed by resealing with plumber's putty or replacing the mounting assembly. A drain-line leak appears lower, at the trap or at the connection between the unit and the sink waste, and it is fixed by tightening or replacing slip joints and washers.

To tell them apart, dry everything, run water into the sink with the plug in and watch where the first drop forms. If the leak only shows when the sink is full, the flange is the likely culprit. If it appears when the sink drains, look lower at the drain parts.

The dishwasher connection

The dishwasher drains through a hose that attaches to a side port on the disposal. If a new disposal is installed, the knockout plug inside that port must be removed first, or the dishwasher will back up into the sink. A clamp that has gone loose or a hose that has cracked leaks at the side of the unit.

Some kitchens use an air gap on the counter or a high loop in the hose to stop dirty sink water from flowing back into the dishwasher. If water bubbles up from the air gap, the drain line past the disposal is usually the clog, and a visit to clear the drain line is the next step.

Clogs that look like disposal trouble

A sink that backs up while the disposal runs fine is often a clog in the trap or the line beyond it. Grease, starchy scraps and coffee grounds build up in a pipe even when the disposal grinds them small. Hard water can also leave mineral scale in drain lines, which narrows them over time.

Hardin County Water District No. 1 (HCWD1) runs the sewer system in Radcliff and describes its supply as hard. The sewer bill follows your water use, so a leaking disposal or a running fixture can inflate it. If a drain stays slow after the trap is cleaned, jetting the line clears it with water pressure.
