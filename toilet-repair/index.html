Most toilet failures come down to a short list of parts. The fill valve lets water in, the flapper lets it out into the bowl, and the wax ring seals the base to the drain flange in the floor. A plumber works through those three in order, and the symptom tells you where to start.

Overflow emergency: what to do first

Do these in order. Each one takes under a minute and each one limits the damage to the floor below.

1

Close the supply valve

Turn the oval handle behind the bowl clockwise. If it spins freely or will not move, go to the next step.

2

Lift the tank lid

Push the rubber flapper down into its seat, or lift the float arm so the tank stops refilling.

3

Stop flushing

Every extra flush pushes more water over the rim if the drain is blocked.

4

Shut the water at the main

If the valve will not close, find the main shutoff for the house and turn it off.

5

Soak up standing water

Towels first, then a wet vac. Water that sits under vinyl or laminate starts lifting it quickly.

The water bill behind a running toilet

Hardin County Water District No. 1 says a toilet that keeps running can waste up to 4,000 gallons a day. That is why a quiet toilet in the middle of the night can turn into a very loud bill. Customers in Radcliff are billed for sewer based on water use too, so the loss shows up twice.

You can test it yourself. Put a few drops of food coloring or a dye tablet in the tank, do not flush, and wait about 15 minutes. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking and the repair is usually a part swap, not a new toilet.

Wax rings, flanges and the floor under them

The wax ring is a thick gasket that seals the toilet outlet to the drain flange. When a toilet has been rocking for months, the seal breaks and slow leaks soak the subfloor. A plumber lifts the toilet, looks at the old ring, then checks the flange for cracks, rust or a height problem.

Flanges in older homes are often cracked or sitting below the finished floor. A repair ring or a new flange fixes that. If the plywood around it is dark or spongy, the floor needs to be repaired before the toilet goes back down, because a new seal on soft wood will rock loose again.

When a new toilet beats another repair

A cracked bowl or a tank that leaks from the china is not repairable. The same goes for a toilet that clogs on most flushes even after the drain is cleared. A fill valve or flapper on a sound toilet is a small repair and worth doing.

Installing a new one means checking the rough-in, which is the distance from the wall to the center of the drain. Most toilets are made for one standard distance, so measuring first avoids a unit that will not fit. The plumber also sets the flange, a new supply line and a working shutoff valve.
