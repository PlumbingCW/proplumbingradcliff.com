Water Meter Running and a High Bill in Radcliff: How to Find the Leak

By the Radcliff Pro Plumbing teamUpdated October 29, 2026

A meter that moves when nothing is on means water is escaping somewhere. A short test tells you whether it is, and where to look next.

Call (855) 575-2890

Run the meter test first

The test takes about an hour, most of it waiting. Do it when no one is using water.

1

Stop all water use

Shut off every fixture and appliance, including the ice maker, dishwasher, washing machine, irrigation and any humidifier on a water line.

2

Find and open the meter box

It is usually near the curb or property line, under a lid you can lift with a screwdriver, and watch for insects or a sharp edge before reaching in.

3

Read it and note the leak indicator

Write down the numbers and look for the small red arrow or dial that spins when any water is moving.

4

Wait 30 to 60 minutes

Do not flush or run a tap during the wait, and keep everyone in the house informed.

5

Read it again

A changed reading, or a red indicator that kept turning, means water is passing the meter while nothing is on.

If the number did not change, the leak may come and go, or the high bill may come from use you have not noticed. If it did change, move on to the next section.

Which side of the meter is the leak on?

Water leaves the meter and passes a shutoff valve at your house. Close the main valve at the house, then check the meter again. If the red arrow stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the buried line between the meter and the house.

That one test cuts the search in half. A leak inside usually shows at a fixture or in a wall or slab. A leak underground may leave a wet or unusually green patch of lawn, a soft spot, or a sound of rushing water near the meter.

Underground problems often lead to a repair of the buried water line, while hidden leaks inside a wall or slab call for acoustic and thermal tracing.

Toilets are the usual suspect

Silent toilet leaks are common because the water runs down the bowl without making a sound. Drop food coloring in the tank and wait without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. Flappers are cheap and many people replace them, but if the problem repeats or the fill valve keeps running, a plumber who does toilet repairs can check the whole mechanism.

Local note

What Radcliff residents can ask HCWD1

Hardin County Water District No. 1 supplies water in Radcliff and operates its sewer system. Its FAQ gives the same meter test described above, with a red leak indicator arrow on the meter, and says a changed reading after 30 to 60 minutes means a leak.

It also lists rough figures. A toilet that runs can waste up to 4,000 gallons a day, a dripping faucet up to 5,000 gallons a month and a steady stream up to 21,000 gallons a month.

The district says it first needs the cause of the usage past the meter found. A qualifying leak may then get an adjusted rate on the non-leak usage, and sewer may be adjusted if the leaked water did not enter the sewer. Sewer bills are calculated from water use. The details are in the utility tariff, so ask HCWD1 before you assume anything, and keep your repair paperwork.

How plumbers find a hidden leak

When fixtures are dry and the meter still moves, the work becomes detective work. These are the methods used.

  • A pressure test on the lines to confirm whether water is escaping.
  • Acoustic listening with ground microphones or contact sensors that hear the hiss of water.
  • Thermal imaging to find warm spots from a hot water line leaking under a floor.
  • Isolating sections with valves to narrow the search to one run of pipe.
  • Tracer gas on stubborn cases, where a harmless gas follows the leak to the surface.
  • A small, well-placed opening in drywall or flooring only after the tools point to the spot.

Good tracing avoids tearing out a whole wall to look. Ask the plumber to explain which method they are using and why.

Before we arrive

Shut off, document, then call

If the test says water is leaking and you cannot find it, do this.

  1. Close the main shutoff at the house if water is visibly flooding anything.
  2. Photograph the meter reading before and after the test with the time on your phone.
  3. Photograph any wet drywall, stains or flooring before you dry anything.
  4. Do not open walls or cut into slabs yourself, because the leak may be on a pressurized line near wiring.
  5. Keep the power off in any room with standing water and call the 24/7 line if it is spreading.

DIY versus a licensed plumber

You can handle the meter test, the dye test for toilets, a visual check under sinks and the swap of a flapper. Those cost almost nothing and often find the cause.

Call a plumber for a meter that moves with the fixtures dry, a wet spot in the yard, a damp wall, warm floor spots, or a relief valve that runs. Those leaks sit on pressurized lines and tend to get worse. A line that fails can become a flood, so waiting for the next bill to confirm is a poor plan.

If you are a landlord or manager and a tenant reports a high bill, run the same test before assuming it is usage.

Insurance and photos

Many homeowner policies cover sudden, accidental water damage but not slow leaks or wear. Ask your insurer how yours treats this, and photograph any damage before cleanup. Keep your meter photos and your bills, since they show when the usage changed.
